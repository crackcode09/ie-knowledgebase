# Module 06 — External Failure Costs

## Objectives

- Identify external failure costs.
- Explain why external failures are the most expensive per event.
- Connect external failure to customer loss.

## What counts as external failure

Costs caused by defects found **after** delivery:

- **Warranty claims** — repairs, replacements, refunds.
- **Returns processing and replacement** — receiving, inspection, restocking, freight both ways.
- **Field service** visits to fix problems at the customer.
- **Complaint handling** — investigation, responses, 8D reports.
- **Concessions and credits** — price reductions, penalties, chargebacks.
- **Product liability and recalls**.
- **Lost customers and lost future sales** — real but usually hidden (Module 07).

| Element | Annual $ | Months 1–6 ($/month) | Months 10–12 ($/month) |

## Why external failures cost more

From the event data (months 1–6, Module 09):

| Detected | Events | Cost | Average per event |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internally | 619 | $600,500 | $970 |
| By the customer | 81 | $443,200 | **$5,472** |

Only 12 % of failure events escaped to customers, yet they carried **42 %** of failure cost. A failure that escapes adds freight, field labor, administration, credits — and damages the relationship.

Notice which drivers escape most: **rod seal leaks (24 % reach the customer)** and **supplier rod plating (19 %)** — failures that show up in use, not at final test. They are prime candidates for better detection (Module 04) **and** prevention (Module 03).

## The customer dimension

External failure costs in the ledger understate the damage. Customers who experience failures may reduce orders, demand price cuts, or audit you more. Ellison's sales manager estimates **$420,000/yr** of contribution lost from two customers who cut orders after quality problems — far larger than the booked complaint-handling cost, and carried in the hidden-cost estimate (Module 07).

## Check yourself

1. Why might external failure costs lag behind internal failures by months?
2. A warranty claim is paid by the dealer and charged back a quarter later. Which month should it be recorded in for CoQ trend analysis?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Products are in distribution and use before failures appear; claims are processed and booked later.
2. For trends, record it when the cost is incurred/charged, but also tag it with the **production period** so you can link it to process changes. Be consistent.
