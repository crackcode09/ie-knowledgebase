# Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Allowance** | Time added for personal needs, fatigue and unavoidable delay during the working day |
| **Attrition** | Separations (quits, retirements, transfers) as a share of headcount |
| **Available hours** | Paid hours minus non-available time |
| **Back-test** | Running the model on past data and comparing with actual results |
| **Coverage model** | Staffing based on posts that must be filled, × relief factor |
| **Delphi** | A structured method of collecting expert estimates over several rounds, with feedback, toward consensus |
| **Demand (staffing)** | Staff required to do the expected workload at the target service level |
| **Erlang C** | Queueing formula (M/M/c) for the probability that an arrival must wait |
| **FTE** | Full-time equivalent |
| **Gap** | Supply minus demand |
| **Human capital management (HCM)** | Planning, acquiring, developing, deploying and evaluating the workforce to meet the mission |
| **Indirect time** | Necessary work not tied to individual workload units |
| **Mission-critical occupation** | A role where too few staff directly harms the organization's mission |
| **Monte Carlo** | Simulation that varies uncertain inputs at random to get a distribution of results |
| **Non-available time** | Paid time not at work: holidays, leave, training |
| **Relief factor** | FTE needed to keep one post covered |
| **Service level** | A measurable promise, e.g. 80 % within 10 minutes |
| **Span of control** | Number of staff per supervisor |
| **Staffing model** | A model that converts workload and policies into required staff by role |
| **Staffing ratio** | Required FTE ÷ FTE on staff |
| **Supply (staffing)** | Staff available after attrition and hiring, adjusted for productivity |
| **Tornado chart** | Bar chart of how much a result changes for each input's range |
| **Unit time** | Time to complete one workload unit by the defined method |
| **Work sampling** | Random observations used to estimate the share of time spent on each activity |
| **Workload driver** | A countable event that creates work |
| **Workforce planning** | The process of matching workforce supply to future demand |
