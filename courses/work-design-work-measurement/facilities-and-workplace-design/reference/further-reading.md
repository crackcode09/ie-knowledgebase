# Further Reading

The course author listed these titles from general knowledge. Editions and details were **not verified** when this list was written, so check the current editions before buying.

- Muther, R. *Systematic Layout Planning*. (The origin of the P, Q, R, S, T inputs, the A–E–I–O–U–X closeness scale and the relationship diagram.)
- Tompkins, J. A., White, J. A., Bozer, Y. A., and Tanchoco, J. M. A. *Facilities Planning*. Wiley.
- Heragu, S. S. *Facilities Design*. CRC Press.
- Hyer, N., and Wemmerlöv, U. *Reorganizing the Factory: Competing Through Cellular Manufacturing*. Productivity Press.
- Rother, M., and Harris, R. *Creating Continuous Flow*. Lean Enterprise Institute.
- Freivalds, A., and Niebel, B. W. *Niebels' Methods, Standards, and Work Design*. McGraw-Hill (motion economy, workplace design).
- NIOSH, *Applications Manual for the Revised NIOSH Lifting Equation*.

## Unverified points and course conventions

- Space multipliers (×2.5 work allowance, ×1.25 aisles, ×2 for warehouse aisles) are course rules of thumb.
- The closeness weights (A 4 … X −4) and the flow bands (A ≥ 90 %, E ≥ 75 %, I ≥ 40 % of the largest flow) are course conventions; texts use several variants.
- The normal and maximum reach distances and the work-height guidance (Module 09) are typical values from general knowledge. Check them against a current ergonomics standard and your workforce.
- The 1.2 m/s walking speed is a planning assumption.
- The handling cost per load-metre ($0.012), the gainsharing figures and the 90 % availability are illustrative.
