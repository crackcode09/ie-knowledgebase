# Module 12 — Ergonomics · Motion Economy

Outline items: **Ergonomics**, **Motion economy**

## Objectives

- Apply ergonomic principles to cell and workstation design.
- Use the principles of motion economy to cut wasted motion.

## Ergonomics

Design the work to fit people, not the other way round. In layout and cell design:

| Risk factor | Design response at Corvan |
|---|---|
| Lifting from the floor | Blanks delivered on a lift table at waist height; finished boxes go onto paint racks at hand height |
| Long or overhead reaches | Parts and tools within the normal work area (Module 09) |
| Heavy repetitive force | Hardware press, not a hand tool; balancers for weld guns |
| Awkward posture | Adjustable-height benches; brake foot pedal positioned for both standing positions |
| Static standing | Anti-fatigue mats; movement between stations in the cell (a benefit of U-cells) |
| Noise | Punch with enclosure or acoustic panels; hearing protection zone marked |
| Fumes | Spot weld extraction; welding separated from paint (the X rating) |

For lifting tasks, use a recognized assessment method such as the NIOSH lifting equation. For other risks, use your organization's ergonomic risk assessment. This table is a design checklist, not an assessment.

## Motion economy

Classic principles of motion economy, grouped:

| Group | Principles |
|---|---|
| Use of the body | Both hands working at the same time in symmetrical motions; use the lowest body member that can do the job (finger before wrist, before arm); use momentum and smooth curved motions; avoid sudden changes of direction |
| Workplace | A fixed place for tools and materials; materials and tools close to and in front of the operator; gravity feed and drop delivery; good lighting; correct height |
| Tools and equipment | Jigs and fixtures instead of holding; combined tools; foot-operated controls to free the hands; tools pre-positioned |

Applied in the cell:

- **Fixtures** hold the box for spot welding, so both hands load.
- **Drop delivery** after the hardware press feeds the inspection station.
- **Pre-positioned tools** sit on balancers.
- **Kanban containers** are sized so the operator never bends to the floor.

## Check yourself

1. Which motion-economy principle does a foot pedal on the hardware press apply?
2. Why does a U-shaped cell help ergonomically as well as for productivity?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Use foot-operated controls to free the hands. Both hands can then hold and position the part.
2. Operators move between stations, which avoids static standing and adds posture variety. Short walks replace long carries.
