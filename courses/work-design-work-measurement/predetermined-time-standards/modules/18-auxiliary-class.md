# Module 18 — The Auxiliary Class

## Objectives

- Explain what the auxiliary class covers and why it is separate from movements and terminals.

## What "auxiliary" means

Movement and terminal codes describe **hand and arm** work. Real tasks also involve:

- the **whole body**: walking, bending, sitting, standing
- the **feet**: pedals
- the **eyes and mind**: focusing, reading, deciding
- **force without motion**: pressing, seating, snapping
- **handling factors**: transferring between hands, cranking, weight

Public descriptions of MODAPTS include activities such as walking and reading as separately coded items ([Eisbrenner PG](https://eisbrennerpg.com/about-modapts/)). The teaching card groups them in its auxiliary class.

## Where auxiliary codes go

- **Body motions** (walk, bend, sit, stand) stand alone on their own line, or before the hand motions they enable.
- **Eye and mental** codes go where the attention is actually needed: before a decision, or before a high-control activity that needs a separate eye movement.
- **Pressure, crank and transfer** go after the movement or put they follow.
- **Load factor** is added to each loaded movement.

## Why the auxiliary class matters

In the carton-packing task, auxiliary codes are **70 of 128 U (55 %)** of the current method: walking, bending and reading. That is where the improvement lies (Module 20). Analysts who code only hand motions miss the biggest savings and the biggest ergonomic risks.

## Check yourself

Which teaching codes would you expect in a task where an operator walks to a rack, stoops to a low shelf, reads a bin label and carries a heavy part back?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

W6 per step (both ways), B22 (stoop and arise), R2 per word or number group, D3 (is it the right bin?), plus the hand codes for the get and L1 on each loaded movement.

</details>
