# Module 15 — The Element GET

## Objectives

- Choose the right get class from what you see.
- Identify ways to cut get time.

## Get classes on the teaching card

| Code | U | Use when | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G0** | 0 | Control by **contact only**: the fingers don't close | Push a button, slide a carton, tap a switch, push a part along a surface |
| **G2** | 2 | **Simple** grasp: an easy-to-hold object, isolated, low control | Pick a housing from a tote; grasp a tool handle; take a carton |
| **G5** | 5 | **Selective** grasp: the eyes must guide the fingers | One screw from a mixed bin; a thin film bag from a stack; a tangled spring; a fragile part |

## Decision steps

1. Do the fingers close on the object? **No** → G0.
2. Must the eyes guide the fingers to separate, select, orient or protect the object? **Yes** → G5.
3. Otherwise → G2.

## Reducing get time

| Cause of G5 | Fix | New code |
|---|---|---|
| Parts mixed or tangled | Separate bins; trays with one part per pocket | G2 |
| Small parts in a pile | Gravity feed or shallow tray that presents single parts | G2 |
| Thin, flat items (bags, labels) | Dispenser with a pull tab | G0 or G2 |
| Fragile item | Fixture or nest with finger clearance | G2 |

## Check yourself

1. Code the get for: picking a carton flap to open it; pushing a part into a nest with the fingertips; taking one rubber band from a bag of bands.
2. Fifteen hundred kits a day each contain three selective gets. How many seconds a day are saved by presenting the parts for a simple get?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Carton flap: G2. Push into nest: G0 for the get (the push itself is a movement plus a put). Rubber band from a bag: G5 (tangled, selective).
2. 3 × (5 − 2) U = 9 U = 0.9 s per kit; × 1,500 = **1,350 s ≈ 22.5 min a day**.

</details>
