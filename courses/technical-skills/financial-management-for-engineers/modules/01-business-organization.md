# Module 01 — Business Organization

## Objectives

- Compare the common legal forms of business and their trade-offs.
- Explain responsibility centers: cost, revenue, profit and investment centers.
- Know which numbers a profit-center manager is accountable for.

> This module describes general features. Legal and tax treatment differs by country and state and changes over time. It is not legal or tax advice — check with your company's finance or legal staff.

## Legal forms

| Form | Owners | Liability of owners | How profit is taxed (general pattern) | Raising capital |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | one person | unlimited, personal | on the owner's personal return | owner's money, personal loans |
| Partnership | two or more | general partners unlimited; limited partners limited | passed through to partners | partners' capital, loans |
| Limited liability company (LLC, US) | members | limited | usually passed through; can elect otherwise | members' capital, loans |
| Corporation | shareholders | limited to investment | entity pays tax; dividends taxed again to shareholders ("double taxation"), unless a pass-through election applies (e.g. US S-corporation) | can sell stock to many investors, issue bonds |

Trade-offs to remember:

- **Liability:** limited-liability forms protect owners' personal assets from business debts.
- **Taxes:** pass-through avoids entity-level tax; corporations may reinvest at the corporate rate.
- **Capital:** only corporations can raise equity at scale (public or private stock offerings).
- **Continuity and transfer:** corporate shares transfer easily; a proprietorship ends with the owner.

Riverbend is a corporation with a handful of private shareholders.

## Inside the company: responsibility centers

Large organizations divide into units, each accountable for the numbers it controls.

| Center | Manager controls | Judged on | Example at Riverbend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost center | costs | spending vs budget, cost per unit | maintenance, quality lab |
| Revenue center | sales | revenue vs target | regional sales office |
| Profit center | revenues **and** costs | operating profit, margin | **Cell 7** (sells machined parts at transfer prices) |
| Investment center | revenue, cost **and** capital | return on investment, residual income | the bearings division |

**Transfer price:** when Cell 7 "sells" parts to Riverbend's assembly lines, an internal price sets its revenue. A transfer price that is too low makes Cell 7 look unprofitable even if it is efficient. Common bases: market price, full cost plus markup, or negotiated.

**Controllable vs allocated costs.** Cell 7's manager controls labor, material, tooling and cell overhead. Corporate overhead allocated by floor space or headcount is not controllable at the cell level; good reports show it separately.

## Organization of the finance function

- **Controller** — accounting, budgets, internal reporting, cost accounting.
- **Treasurer** — cash, banking, borrowing, investing surplus funds.
- **CFO** — both, plus strategy, capital structure and investor relations.

Knowing who owns what speeds up every capital request.
