# Module 09 — Accelerating the Rate of Adoption for Implementing FP&A and EPM

## Objectives

- Recognize the technical, perception, model-design and cultural barriers to EPM adoption.
- Plan actions that overcome resistance to change and fear of accountability.

## Barriers

| Type | Examples | Counter-measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical** | Data scattered across systems; poor driver data; tool limitations | Start with existing data and a spreadsheet model; fix data where decisions need it; automate later |
| **Perception** | "It's a finance project"; "ABC is too complex"; "We already know our costs" | Show one decision it changes (the web-channel analysis); involve operations from day one |
| **Model design** | Too detailed (hundreds of activities) or too coarse; drivers people don't trust | Build for the decisions: 10–30 activities; drivers that operations recognize; time-driven ABC to cut maintenance |
| **Cultural** | Fear of accountability ("the numbers will be used against me"); silo politics; incentives tied to old measures (absorption profit, Module 06) | Use results for learning before evaluation; align incentives with scorecard KPIs; leaders model fact-based decisions |

## Overcoming resistance to change

1. **Executive sponsor**, who must actually use the results in decisions.
2. **Start small, show value fast.** One business question, one pilot (customer profitability for one channel), results in weeks.
3. **Co-design** with the people whose costs are modelled. They own the drivers and the explanations.
4. **Transparency.** Publish the methods, drivers and assumptions. No black boxes.
5. **Separate learning from blame** at first. Ask "what does this tell us?" before "who is responsible?"
6. **Change incentives** that reward the old behaviour.
7. **Build capability**: analytics skills (the scorecard KPI "analytics-skilled analysts", 4 vs a target of 6).
8. **Iterate.** Rolling forecasts and models improve each quarter. Perfection isn't the entry ticket.

## Adoption roadmap (example)

| Quarter | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Sponsor; strategy map; first scorecard; customer profitability pilot (web channel) |
| Q2 | ABC/M for all customers; act on 2–3 decisions; quarterly rolling forecast started |
| Q3 | Driver-based operational budget; risk register linked to the forecast |
| Q4 | Scorecard reviews integrated with forecasts; incentives aligned; post-implementation review |

## Check yourself

1. A sales manager fears that customer-profitability reports will cut his bonus. What do you do?
2. Why start with one business question rather than a full enterprise model?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Involve him in designing the drivers. Use the first results to find actions (pricing specials, visit planning) rather than to evaluate him. Agree with leadership how incentives will change, and when, before results are published.
2. A quick, credible win builds belief and sponsorship. Large models take long, invite arguments about detail, and often stall before they change any decision.
