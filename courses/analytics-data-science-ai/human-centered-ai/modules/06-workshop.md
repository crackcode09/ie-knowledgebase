# Module 06 — Workshop: From People to Prototype

A 30–40 minute team exercise to run at the end of a 2-hour session, or on your own work.

## Steps

1. **Interview (10 min).** Pair up. Interview your partner about the last time a task at work was slow or frustrating (`templates/interview-guide.md`). Capture 6–8 notes.
2. **Themes (8 min).** Combine notes from three pairs; group them silently; name 2–3 themes as sentences.
3. **Frame (5 min).** Write one "How might we…" per theme; pick one with dot voting.
4. **Assumptions (5 min).** List 4–6 assumptions; place them on importance vs evidence; circle the top two.
5. **Prototype plan (7 min).** For each circled assumption, choose the cheapest prototype and define success before testing (e.g. "at least 8 of 12 complete the task in under 2 minutes, and at least 8 of 12 say they would use it daily").

## Skill check

1. State three HCD principles and why they matter for AI adoption.
2. Give two rules for good interview questions.
3. What is the difference between divergent and convergent thinking in synthesis?
4. Write a well-scoped HMW for a problem in your area.
5. What does a Wizard-of-Oz prototype test, and why use it before building AI?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. For example: understand users, tasks and environment (tools must fit the real work); involve users throughout (co-ownership drives adoption); iterate with user evaluation (catches trust and fit problems early).
2. Ask about specific past events; ask open, non-leading questions. (Also: dig with "why", and watch for workarounds.)
3. Divergent: collect and spread all observations without judging. Convergent: group them into a few named themes supported across people.
4. Answers vary. Check that it names the user, the situation and the outcome, and no technology.
5. Whether people will use and value the interaction, with a human producing the answers behind the scenes. It is fast and cheap, and it de-risks the biggest assumption before any AI is built.

</details>
