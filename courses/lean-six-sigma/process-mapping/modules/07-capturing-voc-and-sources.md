# Module 7 — Capturing the Voice of the Customer: Methods and Sources

**Time:** 35 min

## Learning objectives

1. Choose VOC methods appropriate to the question.
2. Identify sources of customer requirements.

## 1. Methods

| Method | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| One-on-one interviews | Discovering needs, the "why" | Small samples; interviewer bias |
| Observation (go to the customer's site) | Unspoken needs, real usage | Access, time |
| Focus groups | Reactions, idea generation | Groupthink |
| Surveys | Measuring how common and how important known needs are | Only measure what you ask; low response; leading questions |
| Complaint and return data | Real problems, frequency, cost | Only hears from unhappy customers who bother to complain |
| Front-line staff (service, sales) | Fast, rich anecdotes | Second-hand, filtered |

**Sequence:** listen broadly (interviews, complaints, front line) → organize (affinity diagram, Module 8) → quantify (survey) → translate (CTQ tree, Module 9) → specify (Module 10).

## 2. Where to go for customer requirements

| Source | RMA example |
|---|---|
| Contracts, quality agreements, purchase order terms | Customer quality manual requires a failure report within 10 working days |
| Industry standards and regulations | Warranty law, product safety reporting |
| Complaint logs, CRM notes, call recordings | "Nobody tells us where our part is" (most frequent theme) |
| Warranty and return data | Return volumes and reasons by customer |
| Lost-business reasons from sales | "Competitor offers 5-day exchange" |
| Benchmarks of competitors and other industries | Advance-exchange programs |
| Direct VOC (interviews, surveys) | Turnaround survey (Module 10) |

## 3. Good VOC practice

- Record **verbatim** statements — the customer's words, not your interpretation.
- Ask about problems and outcomes ("What happens when a carriage is out for repair?"), not solutions ("Do you want a portal?").
- Capture who said it (segment) and how strongly.

## Check yourself

Why isn't complaint data enough on its own?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It only captures customers who were unhappy enough to complain, misses silent defectors, and rarely reveals delighters or the importance of needs that are being met.
</details>
