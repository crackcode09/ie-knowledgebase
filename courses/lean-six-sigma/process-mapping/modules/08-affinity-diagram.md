# Module 8 — Affinity Diagram

**Time:** 30 min

## Learning objectives

1. Use an affinity diagram to organize verbatim VOC into themes.
2. Identify the critical-to-quality (CTQ) needs behind the themes.

## 1. Method

The affinity diagram (also KJ method, after Jiro Kawakita):

1. Write one verbatim statement per card.
2. Team sorts cards **silently** into groups that feel related — no discussion yet.
3. Discuss and refine; a card can be duplicated if it belongs in two groups.
4. Write a **header** for each group that states the underlying need in a short sentence.
5. Optionally group headers into higher-level themes.

## 2. RMA verbatims → themes

| Verbatim statements (24 interviews and complaint notes, selection) | Header (need) |
|---|---|
| "Two weeks is too long — the machine is down." · "We had to buy a spare because the repair took so long." · "Competitor does an exchange in five days." | **Get our machine running again quickly** |
| "No idea where the part is." · "I emailed three times for an update." · "Nobody tells us when it shipped." | **Know the status without asking** |
| "The form asks for things we don't know." · "We sent it back twice because the RMA number was missing." · "Too many emails to get an RMA." | **Make it easy to start a return** |
| "We need to know why it failed for our own report." · "The report said 'wear' — that's useless." | **Tell us the real cause** |
| "Credit took two months." · "Invoice for a warranty repair." | **Get the money right** |
| "The repaired one failed again in a month." | **Fix it right the first time** (basic need) |

These six headers are the customer needs that feed the CTQ tree (Module 9).

## 3. Tips

- Silent sorting prevents the most senior person from deciding the categories.
- Keep the customer's words on the cards; write the header in plain customer language.
- Count cards per group as a rough signal of how often a need came up — but importance comes from a survey, not the card count.

## Check yourself

Why sort silently?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

To let patterns emerge from the data rather than from the loudest or most senior person's assumptions.
