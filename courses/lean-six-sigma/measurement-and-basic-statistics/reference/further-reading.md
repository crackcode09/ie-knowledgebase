# Further Reading

| Source | Why read it |
|---|---|
| NIST/SEMATECH *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*, Ch. 1 "Exploratory Data Analysis". https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ | Free, authoritative: histograms, box plots, measures of location and scale |
| Juran, J. M., & De Feo, J. A. *Juran's Quality Handbook*, 7th ed. McGraw-Hill, 2016. | The Pareto principle in quality, "vital few and useful many" |
| Wheeler, D. J. *Understanding Variation: The Key to Managing Chaos*, 2nd ed. SPC Press, 2000. | Short, practical book on why averages and two-point comparisons mislead |
| Anscombe, F. J. (1973). "Graphs in Statistical Analysis." *The American Statistician*, 27(1), 17–21. | Why you always plot the data (Module 5) |
| Tukey, J. W. *Exploratory Data Analysis*. Addison-Wesley, 1977. | Origin of the box plot and robust summaries |
| Savage, S. L. *The Flaw of Averages*. Wiley, 2009. | Why plans built on averages fail (Module 8) |
| AIAG. *Measurement Systems Analysis (MSA) Reference Manual*, 4th ed. 2010. | Bias, repeatability, reproducibility, resolution (Module 4) |
