# Module 0 — What Is Industrial Engineering? Definition and History

**Time:** 75 min · **Day 1**

## Learning objectives

1. Define industrial engineering (IE) and describe what industrial engineers do.
2. Trace the history of IE from scientific management to today.
3. Navigate this course and its running case.

## 1. Definition

The field's widely used professional definition, paraphrased: industrial and systems engineering is concerned with the **design, improvement and installation of integrated systems of people, materials, information, equipment and energy**, drawing on mathematics, the physical and social sciences, and engineering analysis to **specify, predict and evaluate** the results those systems will produce.

In plain words: other engineers design the product or the machine; **industrial engineers design how the whole system works** — the methods, the workplaces, the flow, the staffing, the measures — so it's productive, safe and good for the people in it.

## 2. What IEs do

| Area | Typical work | Course module |
|---|---|---|
| Methods and work design | Analyze and improve how tasks are done | 2, 3 |
| Work measurement | Set time standards; plan capacity and staffing | 4, 5 |
| Ergonomics and workplace design | Fit the job to the person; prevent injuries | 6 |
| Facilities | Layout, material flow, space | 7 |
| People systems | Job analysis, evaluation, compensation, skills | 8, 9, 10 |
| Process improvement | Lean, Six Sigma, root cause analysis | 11 |
| Data and analytics | Statistics, simulation, optimization | 12 |

IEs work in manufacturing, hospitals, logistics, banks, government agencies, theme parks — anywhere work flows through a system.

## 3. History

| Period | People | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1776 | Adam Smith | Division of labor (*The Wealth of Nations*) |
| 1830s | Charles Babbage | Cost of skills; analysis of manufacturing operations |
| 1880s–1910s | **Frederick W. Taylor** | Scientific management: time study, standard methods, *The Principles of Scientific Management* (1911) |
| 1900s–1920s | **Frank and Lillian Gilbreth** | Motion study, therbligs, fatigue study; Lillian a pioneer of industrial psychology |
| 1910s | Henry Gantt | Gantt chart; task-and-bonus wage plan |
| 1913 | Henry Ford | Moving assembly line |
| 1920s–30s | Walter Shewhart; Elton Mayo | Statistical quality control; Hawthorne studies (human relations) |
| 1940s–50s | Operations research; Deming, Juran | Optimization, quality management |
| 1950s–80s | Taiichi Ohno, Shigeo Shingo | Toyota Production System — the basis of Lean |
| 1980s–today | Motorola, GE; digital tools | Six Sigma; simulation, analytics, automation, AI |

The field has moved from "one best way" set by experts toward systems designed **with** the people who do the work.

## 5. Running case

The **kit-packing department** of an industrial-parts maker: 7 packers per shift pick parts from bins, inspect, bag and box service kits; demand is 5,200 kits/day over two shifts. Management asks the new IE: *How many people do we really need? Why are people waiting? Is the box lift safe? Is the layout right? Are we paying jobs fairly?* All numbers come from `python tools/ie.py all`.
