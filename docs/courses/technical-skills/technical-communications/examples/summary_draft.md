Following the initiation of the DMAIC project on Line 3 centerless grinding operations, an extensive investigation utilizing a multitude of statistical methodologies, including multi-vari analysis, gauge repeatability and reproducibility evaluations, and regression modeling of coolant concentration against outer-diameter deviation, was conducted by the project team in order to determine the predominant contributing factors responsible for the elevated scrap levels that have historically been experienced. It was subsequently determined that the interaction between insufficiently controlled coolant concentration and an inadequately optimized wheel dressing frequency was the principal mechanism underlying the dimensional nonconformances and thermal damage phenomena. Implementation of an automated concentration-control apparatus in conjunction with a revised dressing interval and closed-loop in-process gauging resulted in a statistically significant reduction in the scrap percentage, with a p-value considerably below the conventional threshold of significance. Consideration should potentially be given to the replication of these countermeasures across the remaining grinding lines, contingent upon the availability of capital and engineering resources.
