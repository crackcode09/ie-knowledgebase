# Module 04 — Listening and Feedback from Your Audience

## Objectives

- Use active listening before, during and after a presentation.
- Answer questions and objections clearly and honestly.
- Gather feedback that improves the next presentation.

## Listening before you present

The best presentations are shaped by conversations held beforehand.

- **Pre-wire** the decision: show the key people the message and the numbers one-on-one. Surprises in the meeting cause delay; questions answered in advance don't.
- **Ask, then shut up.** "What would stop you from approving this?" The answer is your next slide or your appendix.
- **Find the skeptic** and understand their concern. A finance manager who doubts the savings figure needs the math, not more enthusiasm.

In the running case, the engineer showed the finance manager the savings math the day before. He asked how she knew Lines 1, 2 and 4 had the same scrap level — so she added the line-by-line scrap history to the appendix.

## Listening during the presentation

**Read the room.** Signs you're losing people: checking phones, side conversations, a decision maker's frown, the same question asked twice. Responses: pause, ask "Is this the level of detail you want?", skip ahead to the ask.

**Active listening for questions:**

1. **Let the person finish.** Don't start answering the question you expect.
2. **Clarify if unsure:** "Do you mean the cost to install, or the running cost?"
3. **Answer briefly, then stop.** Start with the direct answer (yes / no / the number), then one reason.
4. **Check:** "Does that answer it?"

## Handling questions and objections

| Situation | Approach | Example |
|---|---|---|
| You know the answer | direct answer, one supporting fact, offer detail | "Yes — Line 4 uses a different wheel, so we'll pilot there for two weeks first." |
| You don't know | say so; commit to a time | "I don't know the coolant cost per line. I'll have it to you by Thursday." |
| It's an objection | acknowledge, give evidence, restate the benefit | "It's fair to doubt a one-line result. It has held 13 weeks, and the causes are the same machines and practices on all four lines." |
| It's off-topic | park it | "Good question for the maintenance review — can I take it offline?" |
| Hostile tone | respond to the content, not the tone; stay calm and factual | — |

**Never bluff.** A wrong answer given confidently damages credibility more than "I'll find out." Prepare with `templates/qa-prep.md`: list the ten questions you most fear and write a two-sentence answer for each.

## Getting feedback

You cannot judge your own presentation from the inside. Build feedback into the process:

- **Rehearse with a test audience** that resembles the real one — at least one person who doesn't know the project.
- **Use a rubric** (`templates/feedback-rubric.md`) so reviewers score the same things: clear objective, audience fit, structure, data clarity, delivery, Q&A.
- **Ask for comments, then group them** to find the vital few (Module 06 shows a Pareto of comments).
- **After the real event**, ask one or two attendees: "What was clear? What would you have cut?" and note whether the meeting achieved its purpose (decision made?).

## Giving feedback to others

Be specific and actionable, and describe the effect on you: "On slide 3, I didn't know which line was 'after' — a label on the chart would fix it" beats "the chart was confusing." Mention what worked, so it is kept.
