# Module 03 — Prevention Costs

## Objectives

- Identify prevention activities and their costs.
- Explain why prevention has the highest leverage.
- Judge whether prevention spending is effective.

## What counts as prevention

Activities that stop defects from being created:

- **Quality planning and engineering** — control plans, process design, capability studies before launch.
- **Design reviews and FMEA** — finding failure modes on paper.
- **Training** in quality methods and in the job itself (standard work).
- **Supplier quality development** — supplier selection, audits aimed at improvement, joint problem solving.
- **Error-proofing** design and installation.
- **Preventive and predictive maintenance** of quality-critical equipment.
- **Process control** design (SPC system setup, reaction plans).
- **Quality improvement project** time.

## Ellison: prevention before and after the program

| Element | Months 1–6 ($/month) | Months 10–12 ($/month) |
|---|---|---|
| Quality planning & engineering | 9,050 | 15,130 |
| Preventive maintenance (quality-critical) | 4,070 | 6,800 |
| Supplier quality development | 1,850 | 5,950 |
| Training in quality methods | 1,360 | 5,200 |
| Design & process FMEA / reviews | 1,220 | 4,290 |
| **Total prevention** | **17,545** | **37,363** |

Prevention roughly **doubled** (+$19,800/month). Over the same period the failure categories fell by about **$51,000/month** and appraisal by about $8,300/month (Module 11).

## Why prevention has leverage — the "1-10-100" idea

A common rule of thumb says a defect costs about 1 unit to prevent, 10 to find and fix internally and 100 to fix after it reaches the customer. The exact ratio varies, but Ellison's own data shows the pattern (Module 09): an internally found failure averaged **$970**, an externally found one **$5,472** — **5.6× more**.

## Is prevention spending effective?

Prevention is easy to increase and hard to evaluate. Test it:

- **Is it aimed?** Prevention linked to the top cost drivers (Module 09) pays; generic training often doesn't.
- **Is there a lag?** Failure costs typically fall months after prevention starts — Ellison's failure costs began falling in month 8 and kept falling through month 12.
- **Does failure cost fall by more than prevention rises?** That is the test of the whole program.
