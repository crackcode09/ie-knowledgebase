# Module 12 — Capstone and Skill Check

## Part A — Build a CoQ picture for your organization (or a process you know)

1. **Element list** — adapt `templates/coq-element-checklist.md`; categorize with documented rules.
2. **Assessment** — for 6–12 months, find a source or estimate for each element (Module 08); record assumptions.
3. **Tracker** — enter the data in `templates/coq-tracker.xlsx` (clear the sample data first).
4. **Hidden costs** — estimate at least three, with a basis for each, on a separate sheet.
5. **Drivers** — collect or sample failure events; Pareto by cost.
6. **Projects** — propose two or three projects with savings, payback and measurement method.
7. **Report** — a two-page summary using `templates/coq-report-template.md`.

**Review criteria:** categories applied consistently; sources documented; booked and estimated costs separated; projects tied to drivers; savings measurement agreed with finance.

## Part B — Skill check

1. Classify: supplier audit to help a supplier reduce defects; supplier audit to check conformance; sorting a supplier's bad lot; credit to a customer for a defective shipment.
2. Prevention $50k, appraisal $150k, internal failure $300k, external failure $200k; sales $10M. CoQ % of sales and failure share?
3. Give two hidden costs and how you'd estimate each.
4. A scrapped part: material $40, labor and overhead to that point $25, salvage $5. Value?
5. Why rank drivers by cost rather than frequency?
6. A project costs $30,000 and saves $8,000/month. Payback?
7. Hard or soft: (a) reduced warranty claims; (b) 200 engineering hours freed.
8. Using the quadratic loss with k = $500/mm², mean on target, σ = 0.02 mm: expected loss per unit?
9. Sales up 20 %, CoQ $ up 8 %. CoQ % of sales: better or worse?
10. Name three sections of a CoQ summary report.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Prevention; appraisal; internal failure; external failure.
2. Total $700k = 7 % of sales; failure share = 500/700 = 71 %.
3. E.g. lost sales (customer order history × contribution margin), bottleneck lost capacity (quality downtime × contribution per hour), engineering firefighting (work sampling × loaded cost).
4. 40 + 25 − 5 = $60.
5. Cost reflects the business impact; frequent cheap defects can matter less than rare expensive ones, especially those that reach customers.
6. 30,000 / 8,000 = 3.75 months.
7. (a) hard; (b) soft (unless redeployed to avoid other cost).
8. 500 × 0.02² = $0.20 per unit.
9. Better: 1.08 / 1.20 = 0.90 → CoQ % of sales fell 10 % (relative).
10. Any three of: headline totals and trend; category breakdown; top drivers; project status and savings; hidden-cost estimates; decisions requested.

</details>
