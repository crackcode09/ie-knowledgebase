# Module 04 — Detection (Appraisal) Costs

## Objectives

- Identify appraisal activities and their costs.
- Explain the role and limits of inspection.
- Find opportunities to reduce appraisal safely.

## What counts as appraisal

Measuring and checking whether products, processes and services conform:

- incoming / receiving inspection and testing;
- in-process inspection, first-article inspection;
- final inspection and product testing;
- calibration and maintenance of test and measuring equipment;
- quality audits (product, process, system);
- inspection materials, test samples destroyed, lab costs;
- supplier surveillance aimed at checking (not developing).

## Ellison: appraisal

| Element | Annual $ | Months 1–6 ($/month) | Months 10–12 ($/month) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final test & inspection | 257,190 | 22,160 | 20,170 |
| In-process inspection | 204,630 | 18,490 | 14,960 |
| Incoming inspection | 120,170 | 11,070 | 8,440 |
| Calibration & gauge maintenance | 42,360 | 3,480 | 3,600 |
| Quality audits | 29,570 | 2,540 | 2,290 |
| **Total** | **653,920** | **57,737** | **49,457** |

As supplier development and process control improved, incoming and in-process inspection could be reduced. Calibration and audits stayed level — they protect the measurement system and should not be cut.

## The role and limits of inspection

- Inspection **finds** defects; it does not **prevent** them. Every defect found has already incurred material and labor.
- Inspection is not perfect: visual and attribute inspection typically miss a share of defects (the attribute agreement studies in the Quality Engineering Essentials course show how to measure this).
- Appraisal is necessary where processes are not yet capable, for safety-critical features, and where customers require it.

## Reducing appraisal safely

| Condition | Appraisal reduction |
|---|---|
| Process stable and capable (Cpk ≥ target) with SPC | reduce final inspection frequency; move to audit sampling |
| Supplier with a proven record and process data | skip-lot or ship-to-stock |
| Error-proofing installed | remove the downstream check it replaces |
| Automated in-process gauging | replace manual checks |

Never cut appraisal **before** the process improvement that justifies it — that just moves failures from internal to external, the most expensive category.

## Check yourself

1. A manager proposes cutting final test by 50 % to save $128,000. What do you need to know first?
2. Why did Ellison keep calibration spending flat?

1. Current final-test reject rate and what it catches; process capability and stability for the tested features; whether upstream controls (SPC, error-proofing) now prevent those defects; customer requirements; and the expected change in external failure cost.
