# Module 6 — Introduction to the Voice of the Customer

**Time:** 30 min

## Learning objectives

1. Explain how customer expectations should drive process design.
2. Explain how customer expectations change over time.

## 1. Customers define the process's purpose

A process exists to deliver outputs that customers need. Mapping without the voice of the customer (VOC) optimizes the process for the people inside it. The SIPOC (Module 1) names the customers; VOC tells you what they need from each output.

For the RMA: customers don't care about the failure report format or the approval step. They care about **how long their machine is waiting**, **knowing the status**, **why it failed**, and **not having to chase anyone**.

## 2. Expectations change over time

Noriaki Kano's model (1984) classifies needs:

| Type | Absent | Present | RMA example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic (must-be)** | Very dissatisfied | Taken for granted | Repaired part works |
| **Performance (one-dimensional)** | Dissatisfied | More is better | Turnaround time |
| **Delighter (attractive)** | Not missed | Delighted | Proactive photo + root cause sent with the diagnosis |

Over time, **delighters become performance needs and then basics.** Online tracking of a parcel was a delighter; now its absence irritates. Ten years ago a two-week RMA was normal in this industry; now competitors offer a week, and customers compare.

**Implication:** VOC is not a one-time study. Re-collect it regularly; watch what competitors and other industries have made "normal."

## 3. Customers aren't one group

The OEM's maintenance planner (wants speed and status), quality engineer (wants the failure analysis), and purchasing (wants credit processed correctly) have different requirements from the same process. Segment them.

## Check yourself

Customers never mention "the repaired part should work." Does that mean it isn't important?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

No — it's a basic need, assumed so strongly nobody says it. Failing it causes severe dissatisfaction.
