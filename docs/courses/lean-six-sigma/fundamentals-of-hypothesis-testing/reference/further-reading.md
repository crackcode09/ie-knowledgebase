# Further Reading

Editions are the commonly cited ones; check for newer editions.

| Source | Why read it |
|---|---|
| NIST/SEMATECH *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*, Ch. 7 "Product and Process Comparisons". https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ | Free, authoritative, engineering-oriented; covers nearly every test in this course |
| Wasserstein, R. L., & Lazar, N. A. (2016). "The ASA Statement on p-Values: Context, Process, and Purpose." *The American Statistician*, 70(2), 129–133. | What p-values do and don't mean (Module 4) |
| Montgomery, D. C., & Runger, G. C. *Applied Statistics and Probability for Engineers*. Wiley. | Standard engineering statistics textbook |
| Box, G. E. P., Hunter, J. S., & Hunter, W. G. *Statistics for Experimenters*, 2nd ed. Wiley, 2005. | Comparisons, pairing and blocking, from the people who built the methods |
| Hollander, M., Wolfe, D. A., & Chicken, E. *Nonparametric Statistical Methods*, 3rd ed. Wiley, 2014. | Reference for Modules 15–16 |
| Cohen, J. *Statistical Power Analysis for the Behavioral Sciences*, 2nd ed. 1988. | The classic on power and effect size (Module 3) |
| Cochran, W. G. (1954). "Some methods for strengthening the common χ² tests." *Biometrics*, 10(4), 417–451. | Origin of the expected-count guidance (Module 9) |
| Welch, B. L. (1947). "The generalization of 'Student's' problem when several different population variances are involved." *Biometrika*, 34(1–2), 28–35. | The Welch t-test (Module 11) |
