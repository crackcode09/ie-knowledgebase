# Module 3 — The Source of Value, Gemba Walks, the 3 Ms and 8 Wastes

**Time:** 60 min · **Session 2: Define & Measure**

## Learning objectives

1. Define value from the customer's point of view.
2. Conduct a gemba walk.
3. Recognize the 3 Ms and the 8 wastes.

## 1. Introduction to process mapping and the source of value

A process is a series of steps that turn inputs into outputs for a customer. Mapping makes it visible (Module 4). Before mapping, agree what **value** means:

An activity is **value-added** if (1) the customer is willing to pay for it, (2) it physically changes the product or information toward what the customer wants, and (3) it's done right the first time. Everything else is either **business-required non-value-added** (necessary today, e.g., a regulatory test) or **waste**.

For a manifold: machining ports, deburring them, and assembling valves are value-added. Moving blanks between buildings, waiting in queues, re-machining, and counting inventory are not.

## 2. Gemba walks

*Gemba* — "the real place." Go see the work where it happens.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Follow one product (or document) from start to finish | Stay in the office reviewing reports |
| Ask "show me" and "what gets in your way?" | Blame or fix on the spot without understanding |
| Count inventory, time steps, measure distances | Rely on what the procedure says happens |
| Note waste with photos and facts | Walk with a big entourage that changes behavior |

Leaders' regular gemba walks are also part of leader standard work (Module 20).

## 3. The 3 Ms

| | Meaning | Manifold plant |
|---|---|---|
| **Muda** | Waste | 2,800 units of inventory for a 220/day demand |
| **Mura** | Unevenness | Distributors order in big monthly batches; daily schedule swings roughly 120–340 units (illustrative) |
| **Muri** | Overburden | CNCs scheduled at 100% of available time with no room for changeovers or maintenance |

Mura and muri cause muda: uneven orders overload the CNCs, which break down and make defects. Leveling (heijunka, Modules 7 and 17) attacks mura directly.

## 4. The 8 wastes — DOWNTIME

| Waste | Manifold plant example |
|---|---|
| **D**efects | 8.5% of manifolds fail leak test first time |
| **O**verproduction | Machining families ahead of need to avoid changeovers |
| **W**aiting | Blanks waiting 2.9 days for the CNC |
| **N**on-utilized talent | Operators' ideas for fixtures never acted on |
| **T**ransportation | Blanks moved by forklift between two buildings |
| **I**nventory | 2,800 units = 12.7 days of demand |
| **M**otion | Walking to the tool crib for every changeover |
| **E**xtra-processing | Re-deburring parts that were already acceptable; double data entry |

Ohno listed seven wastes; "non-utilized talent" was added in Western practice. Overproduction is often called the worst, because it creates the others.

## Check yourself

Classify: (a) a forklift moving pallets between buildings; (b) machining more than ordered to avoid a changeover; (c) the leak test required by the customer.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

(a) Transportation waste. (b) Overproduction. (c) Business-required non-value-added (not waste to eliminate today, but to reduce).
