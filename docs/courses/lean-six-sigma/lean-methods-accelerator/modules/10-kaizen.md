# Module 10 — Kaizen

**Time:** 40 min

## Learning objectives

1. Explain daily kaizen and kaizen events.
2. Outline how a kaizen event is planned and run.

## 1. Two forms of kaizen

| | Daily kaizen | Kaizen event (blitz) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Everyone, in their own work area | Cross-functional team, full time |
| Scale | Small improvements, continuously | Focused redesign of one process segment |
| Duration | Minutes to days | 3–5 days plus pre-work and follow-up |
| Tools | Suggestion systems, team boards, PDCA | VSM, takt, flow, SMED, 5S, standard work |

Masaaki Imai's *Kaizen* (1986) brought the term to the West. Both forms matter: events make step changes; daily kaizen sustains and extends them.

## 2. A kaizen event in brief

| Phase | Key activities |
|---|---|
| Pre-work (3–6 weeks) | Charter with measurable goals, team, baseline data, logistics, build-ahead |
| Day 1 | Gemba walk, current state, takt, constraint |
| Day 2 | Root causes, solution ideas |
| Days 3–4 | Implement and try (move equipment, fixtures, SMED, standard work) |
| Day 5 | Standardize, 5S, visual management, report-out |
| Follow-up | 30/60/90-day checks until results hold |

**Manifold plant event:** "CNC changeover kaizen" — goal 90 → 20 min (Module 15); followed by a "cell kaizen" to create the machining cell (Module 14).

For the full method (planning, leading teams, daily schedules, templates) see the *Kaizen Leader* course in this repository.

## 3. PDCA

Every kaizen, daily or event, follows **Plan–Do–Check–Act**: plan from data, try it, check results against the goal, standardize what works (or adjust and repeat).

## Check yourself

A kaizen event cut changeover to 25 minutes, but three months later it's back to 70. What was missing?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sustainment: standard work, visual controls, audits, leader follow-up and daily kaizen to keep and extend the gains.
</details>
