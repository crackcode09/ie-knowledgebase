# Glossary

**3 Ms** — Muda (waste), mura (unevenness), muri (overburden).

**5S** — Sort, set in order, shine, standardize, sustain.

**A3** — One-page problem-solving story.

**Andon** — Signal for help when an abnormality occurs.

**Bullwhip effect** — Amplification of demand variability upstream in a supply chain.

**Cell / cellular processing** — Machines grouped by product family for one-piece flow.

**CONWIP** — Constant work in process: release work only when a job leaves.

**COPQ** — Cost of poor quality: prevention, appraisal, internal and external failure.

**Drum-buffer-rope** — TOC scheduling: constraint sets the pace, protected by a buffer, with release tied to it.

**EPEI** — Every part every interval: how often all products can be run.

**Gemba** — The real place where work happens.

**Heijunka** — Leveling production volume and mix.

**Jidoka** — Built-in quality; stop on abnormality.

**Kaizen** — Continuous improvement; kaizen event = focused improvement workshop.

**Kanban** — Signal that authorizes production or replenishment.

**Leader standard work** — Defined routines for leaders to check and support the system.

**Little's law** — Lead time = WIP ÷ throughput.

**MTBF / MTTR** — Mean time between failures / to repair.

**OEE** — Overall equipment effectiveness = availability × performance × quality.

**PCE** — Process cycle efficiency = value-added time ÷ lead time.

**Pitch** — Pack quantity × takt; the interval for pulling one container.

**Product–process matrix** — Parts × machines table used to find families.

**Pull** — Production triggered by downstream consumption with capped WIP.

**SMED** — Single-minute exchange of die (quick changeover).

**Standard work** — Takt, work sequence, standard WIP.

**Supermarket** — Controlled stock that downstream pulls from.

**Takt time** — Available time ÷ customer demand.

**TOC** — Theory of constraints; five focusing steps.

**TPM** — Total productive maintenance.

**Value stream map** — Material and information flow with a lead-time timeline.

**Y = f(X)** — Outputs are determined by inputs.
