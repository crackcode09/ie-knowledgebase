# Module 7 — Affinity Diagram

**Time:** 35 min

## Learning objectives

1. Organize verbatim VOC into needs with an affinity diagram.

## 1. Method (KJ method, after Jiro Kawakita)

1. One verbatim statement per card — the customer's words.
2. Silent sorting into natural groups (no talking).
3. Discuss; move or duplicate cards.
4. Write a header for each group stating the need in the customer's language.
5. Group headers into themes if helpful.

## 2. Case — 24 interviews, selected verbatims

| Verbatims | Need (header) |
|---|---|
| "The line is down — I need it before the next shift." · "We keep spares we don't need because delivery is unreliable." · "Competitor ships the same day if we order by 3." | **Get the part to me fast and reliably** |
| "I call three times to find out where the order is." · "Nobody tells us it's back-ordered until it's late." · "I want a tracking number without asking." | **Let me see where my order is** |
| "The old part's label is worn off — I don't know what to order." · "Your numbers don't match the OEM's." · "I take a photo and email it to the rep." | **Help me identify the right part** |
| "Wrong size arrived — machine still down." · "Two parts in one box with the same label." | **Send the right part first time** |
| "Distributor price is lower than yours direct." · "Quotes take two days." | **Fair, quick pricing** |
| "When it fails early I want to know why." | **Explain failures** |

These needs become the top level of the CTQ tree (Module 8). Process Mapping, Module 8, shows another worked affinity diagram.

## Check yourself

Why write the header in the customer's language rather than internal terms like "OTIF"?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It keeps the team anchored on what the customer experiences; internal metrics come later, in the CTQ tree, and must trace back to these needs.
</details>
