# Further Reading

The course author listed these titles from general knowledge. Editions and details were **not verified** when this list was written, so check the current edition first.

- Provost, F., and Fawcett, T. *Data Science for Business*. O'Reilly. Covers analytic thinking, classification, evaluation and the business value of models.
- James, G., Witten, D., Hastie, T., and Tibshirani, R. *An Introduction to Statistical Learning*. Springer. Free PDF from the authors. Covers regression, classification, PCA and clustering.
- Hyndman, R. J., and Athanasopoulos, G. *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*. OTexts. Free online. Covers exponential smoothing, accuracy measures and holdout testing.
- Kimball, R., and Ross, M. *The Data Warehouse Toolkit*. Wiley. Covers star schemas and dimensional modeling.
- Kleppmann, M. *Designing Data-Intensive Applications*. O'Reilly. Explains the technology behind Big Data systems.
- Cleveland, W. S., and McGill, R. (1984). "Graphical Perception." *Journal of the American Statistical Association*. The study behind the ranking of visual encodings in Module 01.
- Shneiderman, B. (1996). "The Eyes Have It." The source of "overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand."
- Hillier, F. S., and Lieberman, G. J. *Introduction to Operations Research*. McGraw-Hill. Covers linear programming and sensitivity analysis.
- Chapman, P., et al. *CRISP-DM 1.0 Step-by-step Data Mining Guide* (2000).

The following claims in the modules come from general knowledge and should be checked before citing:

- the "60–80% of effort is data preparation" estimate;
- the "Vs" of Big Data;
- the descriptions of technology categories;
- spreadsheet feature names, which vary by version.
