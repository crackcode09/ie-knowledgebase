Where this is going

Site roadmap · updated 2026-10-27 · the course coverage checklist is a separate thing, see ROADMAP.md
The end goal. You name a position close to industrial engineering that you want to work in, say continuous improvement engineer. The site tells you what to train, how to train it here, and where to focus, grounded in the official description of that role and in what employers currently ask for, including the expectation that engineers in these roles use AI in their work.

What exists today

  • Courses. Original, free courses grouped the way the IISE catalog groups the discipline. The catalog shows what is built and what is still planned in each category.
  • Learning path. Courses arranged by prerequisite, with your progress kept in your own browser.
  • Mind map and reader. The whole map on one screen, and every lesson readable on the site.
  • Trust. Every course carries a draft or verified status. A review board checks originality, accuracy, trademarks and structure, and a named person signs off before a course is called verified.

What the advice will be based on

Three sources, so the advice is evidence rather than opinion:

  1. The official standard for the position. The U.S. Department of Labor's O*NET occupational profiles, starting with Industrial Engineers, 17-2112.00: rated tasks, knowledge, skills, abilities, work activities and technology, with "in demand" flags, education levels and wage and growth figures. Reused under CC BY 4.0 with attribution; O*NET is a trademark of USDOL/ETA and this site is not affiliated with or endorsed by it.
  2. The outlook for the position. The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, starting with Industrial Engineers: pay, entry education and licences, the largest employers, the ten-year growth projection and what drives demand. U.S. government work, cited as the source.
  3. The market signal. What employers currently ask for in postings for the role, including AI and data tooling, which neither source above carries yet. Source still to be chosen.

The IISE catalog gives the map of the discipline. The courses here are the training. The two sources turn that into advice for a named role, and every focus point will cite the source it comes from.

What will not change

  • Original content only; the IISE catalog is a map, not a source.
  • A course stays draft until a named person verifies it.
  • Free and open, no accounts, no tracking.
