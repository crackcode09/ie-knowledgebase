Site roadmap

Where this build is going · updated 2026-10-27 · different from the course roadmap, which lists which IISE topics have a course yet
The end goal. You name a position close to industrial engineering that you want to work in, say continuous improvement engineer. The site tells you what to train, how to train it here, and where to focus, grounded in the official description of that role and in what employers currently ask for, including the expectation that engineers in these roles use AI in their work.
__COURSES__ courses built__VERIFIED__ verified__COVERED__ of __TOTAL__ catalog topics covered1 of 5 stages done

  1. Original courses grouped the way the IISE catalog groups the discipline, a coverage checklist, and a site with a catalog, a prerequisite-ordered learning path, a mind map and an on-site reader with progress kept in your browser.

    • __COURSES__ courses, __COVERED__ of __TOTAL__ catalog topics covered
    • Catalog, learning path, mind map, reader, local progress

  2. Every course was written with AI assistance, so every course carries a draft status until a named person has verified it. A review board checks originality, accuracy, trademarks and structure and lists its doubts for the reviewer; the person signs off.

    • Review board built; __VERIFIED__ of __COURSES__ courses verified so far
    • Privacy policy, terms of use and the originality notice published

  3. One profile per target position. The official standard's tasks, knowledge, skills and technology, ranked by importance, plus what the market asks for now, each mapped to the courses and modules here that teach it. The gaps, the important elements with no module yet, feed the course roadmap.

    • Evidence in hand: the O*NET profile and the BLS outlook for Industrial Engineers (see below)
    • Still to choose: a postings-based market signal for current tooling asks, including AI

  4. Pick a role, say what you already know, and get a training plan through the courses here, the focus points that matter for the role today, and an honest list of what the catalog cannot cover yet. Every focus point cites the source it comes from.

  5. The standards update quarterly and the market moves faster. The sources are refreshed on a schedule and each role profile shows what changed since the last refresh, so the advice ages well.

What will not change

  • Original content only; the IISE catalog is a map, not a source.
  • A course stays draft until a named person verifies it.
  • Free and open, no accounts, no tracking.
